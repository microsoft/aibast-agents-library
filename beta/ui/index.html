Rapid customer use-case accelerator

Answer "Can AI do this?" with a working proof.

RAPP Brainstem is a local prototype-and-test harness. GitHub Copilot builds and repairs the solution; Brainstem makes the behavior visible, repeatable, portable, and easy to test before you decide where it belongs in production.

What is this for?

Turn a customer problem, transcript, screenshot, document, or expected outcome into one or more single-file agents. Run them locally, inspect every tool call, correct failures, and prove the workflow with the customer.

It is the fastest path from an idea to evidence. It is not the final production host.

Who should use it?

Technical sellers, architects, specialists, engineers, partners, and customer builders who can steer a prototype and judge whether it solves the business problem.

It is not the default interface for account executives or end users. They consume the validated outcome in the right production experience.

When should I reach for it?

  • A customer asks, "Can AI do this process?"
  • The requirements are still conversational or ambiguous.
  • You need a demo, feasibility proof, or process specification quickly.
  • You want failures to become concrete evidence Copilot can fix.
  • You need portable agent files that teammates can hot-load and improve.

When should I not use it?

  • Simple employee knowledge or Work IQ tasks already fit Scout.
  • The production design, integrations, governance, and target are already known.
  • You need to put real customer data into an unapproved local prototype.
  • You are treating a local proof as a production-ready solution.

How the tools fit together

RAPP BrainstemUse as the local proving ground: hot-load capabilities, interview the agent, inspect tool calls, and lock in intent.

The rapid customer loop

  1. Frame: capture the problem, users, inputs, outputs, constraints, and success proof.
  2. Build: have Copilot create the smallest useful single-file agent set.
  3. Interview: test through Brainstem like a real user; inspect memory and tool calls.
  4. Correct: export the failed transcript, open VS Code, and tell Copilot to fix and retest.
  5. Package: share the agents, demo, evidence, requirements, and recommended production route.

What should come out?

  • A working, evidence-backed local prototype.
  • Portable single-file agents that document the process deterministically.
  • Known gaps for data, authentication, connectors, security, and governance.
  • A demo story plus design/SOW-ready requirements.
  • A clear route to Scout, Copilot Studio, Foundry, custom code, or "do not proceed."

Start with this prompt

I am working on a rapid customer proof. Interview me until you understand the business problem, users, inputs, expected outputs, constraints, and what would prove success. Then propose the smallest single-file RAPP agent set that can test the idea locally with synthetic or approved data. Build it, run it through Brainstem, show me the evidence, identify production gaps, and recommend whether the final experience belongs in Scout, Copilot Studio, Foundry, or custom code. Do not call the prototype production-ready.
Default posture: builder-operated, local-first, synthetic or approved data, human validation, then governed promotion.
